brigade

What is paid, and to whom.

Brigade charges nothing. Its code is open source (MIT), and it bills no subscription and takes no cut. The only cost you can meet is the machine that runs a cloud station, and that is paid to your own hosting provider.

Who is paid

  • Brigade: nothing. No licence, no seat, no per-ticket fee, no commission on merged work.
  • Your hosting provider: the machine. A cloud station runs on a virtual machine you rent from a hosting provider of your choice. You pay that provider for the machine hours, as you would for any server.
  • Anthropic: your Claude subscription. The cooks run on your own Claude Max session, as they always have.

When the machine runs

A station does not run all the time. It runs only while the rail has servable tickets: while a ticket is fired and waiting, or a cook is still working on one. When the rail is empty, the station is idle and the machine hours stop adding up.

So the cost follows the work. A quiet week costs little; a week with many tickets costs what those tickets take.

You set the ceiling

The ceiling is the limit you choose on what the kitchen may spend. It caps the spend, so a runaway ticket cannot keep a machine busy beyond what you allowed. The kitchen's brakes (caps on duration, tokens and simultaneous cooks, and one stop that halts everything) are how that ceiling is enforced.
